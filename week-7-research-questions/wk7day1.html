Week 7 · First session — Studio

Act II · aim at my question · from field noticing to an inquiry · you were just in the classroom — what do you want to find out?
Today's goals: Make sense of what you saw in the field, and reconnect with the concept you're investigating — the mathematics you're setting out to understand, and the work you collected on it. That is the raw material you'll turn into a research question next class.

1 · A new half of the course starts here (~15 min)
  • For five weeks you practiced the moves — Learner, Listener, Poser, Lesson Planner, Assessor — on tasks we all shared. From here, you point those same moves at one mathematical concept — and the student work that reveals how it's understood.

Where this is heading: Weeks 7–10. Today we begin our Act II project. Over the next four weeks you will take the first-read question from your Concept Profile, sharpen it into a research question this week, and build a Before-During-After lesson to investigate it when you return to the field in Weeks 11 and 12.

  • Before — a short routine that warms up the thinking.
  • During — a rich task that makes students' understanding of the concept visible.
  • After — a culminating piece you can analyze: an exit slip, or a discussion you capture (photograph the board, or have students jot their reasoning). The one rule: come away with an artifact.

By Week 10 you'll have the full plan. In the field, enact as much as your placement allows — one student or a whole class, one session or several; you need not teach every part. The goal: gain insight into student thinking, collect the work that shows it, and report on it in your Final Report.

2 · Field debrief — what do you understand now that you didn't before? (~45 min)
  • With a partner, then as a class: what surprised you in your placement? What was different from what you expected a classroom to be?
  • What did your host teacher point to — a topic that's coming, something students are building right now, something she wishes she had more time for?
  • What did you see students do with the mathematics of your concept? Describe it without judging it yet.
  • Placement worries will surface here. I've compiled the common ones on the Act II questions page: how little of the lesson you have to teach, what to do about a scripted curriculum, what happens when the room is not a math classroom. Take five minutes to look through it, and then we'll talk about whatever it doesn't settle.

Purpose: the debrief is where the field stops being a blur and becomes specific. A research question can only grow out of something specific you noticed.

3 · Back to your concept (~15 min)
  • Return to the concept you named in the field: your Concept Profile and the work you collected. Remind yourself of the mathematics you're setting out to understand and where it's headed.
  • Look at the samples again, with fresh eyes. What do you notice now that you couldn't in the moment?
  • Start a running list of questions and wonderings about how students understand the concept. Don't polish them — you'll work on them next class. Keep the list.
  • No concept yet? Ask your host teacher which upcoming topics or activities lend themselves well to revealing student thinking. That is the fastest way in, and it asks her about the next week or two rather than the next month. Anything bigger is on the Act II questions page; email me with what it doesn't cover. Either way, land on a concept before next class.

Purpose: the concept was chosen in the field — today you reconnect with the collected work and start the raw questions we will sharpen next class.

4 · Where this is heading (~5 min)
  • The question you build next class becomes the spine of the whole back half of the course. The lesson you'll design over the next few weeks (see Section 1) exists to investigate it, so a good question is one that lesson could actually help you investigate.
  • This week's deliverable is the Research Question. I introduce it; you read it tonight, criteria included.

Purpose: you read faster than I talk, and your questions after reading are better than your questions before it.

Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • Read the Research Question assignment, criteria included. Note what row 2 asks of you: three to five samples, read closely.
  • Open the Research Question Studio and set up the first two tabs: load your concept, then describe a piece of the work you collected. There is nothing to upload. Stop there. We run the question storm together next class.
  • Bring the questions and wonderings from today.
  • Our next class is a working session: you'll turn that raw list into one research question, then hand it to a genAI planning partner to sharpen — not to write.
