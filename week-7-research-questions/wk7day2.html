Week 7 · Second session — Clinic

Act II · aim at my question · the Question Formulation Technique · you write the questions; the machine only sharpens them
Today's goals: Turn the pile of wonderings from last class into one research question about how students understand your concept — one your nested lesson could help you answer. You'll storm questions from the work you collected, sharpen them with a genAI planning partner, and land on one.

1 · Questions, then a walk through the Studio (~20 min)
  • Any questions about the Q&A? Additional questions it didn't address?
  • A walk through the Research Question Studio, tab by tab — on the screen, with everyone following along on their own machine. Ask as we go; this is the twenty minutes for it.
    • 1 · The Concept. Your Week 6 concept, brought across from the Planner. Check it still says what you mean.
    • 2 · Collected work. The work on the left, your reading of it on the right, one sample at a time. Describe; do not diagnose.
    • 3 · Brainstorm. The four rules, on your own work. Ten questions before anything can be sorted, and no open/closed until you say you are done — rule 2, built into the page.
    • 4 · Check names. Nothing identifying leaves your machine.
    • 5 · Hand off. One PDF — that is Document 1 — and then across to the Planner.
  • Everything stays in your browser, and you upload nothing. What you type is a description, so keep it de-identified: a pseudonym, never a real name.

2 · One QFT, together (~15 min)
  • We run the protocol once together, on one shared QFocus — work none of you collected, so nobody is defending their own placement. One third grader, one question: show the work. From Cookie Dough, NYC DOE Grade 3 (Achieve/EQuIP); identification and grades redacted by the publisher.
  • The four rules, enforced out loud:
    1. Ask as many questions as you can.
    2. Do not stop to discuss, judge, or answer any question.
    3. Write every question down exactly as it is thought.
    4. Turn any statement into a question.
  • Someone scribes verbatim. Then we mark each one open or closed and prioritize three.
  • Watch what the rules do to you. Rule 2 is the hard one — and the one you cannot enforce on yourself at a laptop.

3 · Build your question in the Studio (~25 min)
  • Same protocol, your own QFocus, your own pace, in the Research Question Studio. Concept and Collected work should already be done — if not, finish them first.
  • When the storm is done, hand off. The Studio makes Document 1 and carries your work into the Planner; it tells you what to press.

4 · Revisit your question in 2c, and sharpen it (~15 min)
  • Your Week 6 question is in front of you, with today's storm under it. Revise it, or carry one of the new ones.
  • Press Check this: it reads your question against your concept and your samples and pushes on it — investigable? about how students reason, or just whether they were right? Show me options widens; Ask me follows up.
  • It will not hand you a question. If it tries, push back.
  • One test throughout: could the nested lesson you build next actually answer this?

Purpose: the move you're learning is AI widens, my criteria narrow. The human noticing is the source of the question; the machine is a way to see more angles on it.

5 · Land one question (~5 min)
  • Down to the one question you carry into the field, plus a sentence or two on why.
  • Test it: open, investigable, and could your nested lesson gather evidence on it?
  • This is half of Document 2 — the landed question and why. Write it down now, while it is in front of you. The other half is the two or three exchanges that moved it, and those are kept under Earlier reads in the Planner.

If you finish early
  • Trade questions with a partner. Ask a question about their collected work that they're too close to see — then let them do the same for you. Both of you keep your own question.
Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • This week's deliverable is the Research Question — the question you land today, plus the record of how you sharpened it (the assignment has the details).
  • Next week you design the first piece of that lesson — a rich task aimed squarely at the question you just wrote.
