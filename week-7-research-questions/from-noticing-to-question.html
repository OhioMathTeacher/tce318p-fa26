From Noticing to a Research Question

TCE 318P · Week 7 · Act II

Six things people noticed in the field. Each one became a question, and then got turned until a lesson you could build would answer it. The first question is never the mistake. It is where every one of these started.

What the guidelines ask of a research question

  1. It asks one thing. Not two or three joined with “and.”
  2. It is open. No single right answer, and not yes or no.
  3. It is about their reasoning, not about whether they got it right.
  4. It names the mathematics, and it is not a verdict on a child.
  5. A lesson you could build would answer it.

Four of these five come straight out of the Concept Profile guidelines: “one specific, grade-appropriate mathematical concept” and “an open question about how students understand this concept, not whether they got it right, and not a diagnosis of a child.” The fifth is the one the rest of Act II depends on, and it is in the Week 7 outline.

Pick a noticing. One box opens at a time.
What she noticedOne of them understood making ten and still wrote the wrong number down. Her strategy was right and her answer was not.
Her first question

How can I help first graders get better at adding within 20?

Turned toward research

How do first-grade students use the make-10 strategy and ten-frames to solve addition problems within 20?

Named the strategy, the tool and the range, so you know what work would answer it.
