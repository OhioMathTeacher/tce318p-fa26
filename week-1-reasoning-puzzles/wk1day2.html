Week 1 · Second session — Clinic

Operations & Algebraic Thinking · Learner · making your own thinking visible
Today's goals: Name what last class was — figuring things out by reasoning, where the struggle was the point — then play all three games in the app, watching your own thinking with playback and mark-up. At the end we'll look at your first Pedagogical Excursion together.

1 · The reading, and how we keep it (~25 min)

  • The article first (~10). McFeetors & Palfy — what resonated with you? Any new ideas? Questions? Then back to the puzzles from last class: how did you figure out the rules, and where was the struggle? Name what that was — inductive reasoning; the struggle was the point; process over answer.
  • Then: let me show you some notes I took (~10). Open Journaler with the article and walk through real margin notes — the passage, what I wrote beside it, and clicking a note to jump back to the page it came from. Then This Week's Entry: the prompt, the passages gathered as evidence, and a response written in your own words.
  • Say the two things plainly. It is completely fine if you have not done this yet — and the Journal is not collected until after Week 5. You can go back and revisit any week's entry across all of Act I. Open Journal and every week shows where it stands.
  • Then you try it (~5). Open the article in Journaler, mark one passage, and write what you notice. That is a note. No AI needed — the reading partner is optional, and off is a fine way to work this week.

Purpose: discuss the reading as a reading, then show — not describe — what keeping it looks like. The notes on screen are real ones, made the night before, because a worked example beats an explanation.

2 · Game 1 — Metadoku, together (~20 min)

  • We'll play Metadoku as a class to learn the app: playback (watch your own solve step by step), mark-up (note what you were thinking), and the report you can download.
  • This is the one we do properly. The process is the same in all three — play, play it back, mark it up — so once it feels familiar you have what you need. Metaplete and Metagons are there if time allows, and on your own afterwards.
  • Debrief: what did watching your own solve show you that you didn't notice while solving?

3 · Game 2 — Metaplete (time permitting)

  • Now on your own: play Metaplete, then play it back and mark up your solving.
  • Debrief: was your thinking different in this game than in Metadoku?

4 · Game 3 — Metagons (time permitting)

  • Same again with Metagons — play, play it back, mark it up.
  • Debrief: across all three games, where did you have a real strategy, and where were you guessing?

5 · Look at the assignment together (~25 min)

  • We'll open Pedagogical Excursion #1 and read through it together — that's the one place the details live.
  • Choose your board. Metadoku or Metaplete need at least 4×4; Metagons needs a square or a pentagon. Pick now, while the games are fresh.
  • Walk the rubric (20 pts): eligible board (3) · annotated decision points (6) · strategy named (5) · learner → teacher reflection (6). The six points for annotation want real moments — where you guessed, backed up, or saw the key step — not "move 1, move 2."
  • Where the report comes from. Download it from the game (.md or PDF, no names in the file) and submit it in Canvas — not in Journaler. Journaler is for the Reading Journal; this is a different assignment with a different home.
  • You'll finish it at home and submit it by Sunday.

If you finish early

  • Look back at your three games — which one do you want to write up for the assignment, and why?
