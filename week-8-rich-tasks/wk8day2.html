Week 8 · Second session — Clinic

Act II · test the task · does it make the thinking visible? · the first time your task meets someone who has never seen it
Today's goals: You brought a task. Today you find out whether it does its job. The person next to you, who has never seen it, will explore it while you watch. You'll compare their thinking with any solutions your source published. Only then will you take it to a genAI planning partner. Human first, machine second.

1 · What your task has to do (~5 min)
  • The task is the main part of your lesson — the During. Its job is to make students' thinking visible: what they write, draw, and say should tell you something about your research question.
  • So today we test two things: does the task show the thinking your question is about, and can a child at your grade get started on it?

2 · Cold test — explore, then decide (~30 min)
  • With the person next to you: you are each other's Task Testers. (If that's your research partner, who already knows your task, pair with someone else.)
  • Round 1 (10 min): Partner A poses their task the way they would to students, hands over the materials, and stops talking. Partner B explores it, thinking aloud. They don't need to finish: the goal is to find out whether the task is rich, not to get the answer. A doesn't rescue, hint, or explain, and photographs B's work.
  • Round 2 (10 min): switch. B poses, A explores aloud, B watches and photographs.
  • Decide together (10 min), one task at a time: Put the tester's work next to any solutions your source published (an NRICH Solutions page, Van de Walle's or YouCubed's teacher notes, the student work in a JSTOR article — or your own solution if there are none). Can you find three really different strategies? Is the task rich enough, and does it fit your research question? Write down what your tester says.

Purpose: the hardest part is not helping. And people rarely solve a task the way you predict, which is exactly what you need to see before you teach it.

3 · Read the assignment together (~10 min)
  • Open the Rich Task assignment and read it together, rubric included. You've just watched your task get explored, so you'll know what each part is asking.
  • Look for what's easy to miss:
    • the Ohio standard your task targets (the first rubric row)
    • where students might get stuck, and what that would tell you
    • three really different strategies, from your Task Tester and any published solutions
    • two documents: your task, and your two-reads reflection
  • Ask your questions now.

4 · Work time (~35 min)
  • Open the BDA Planner, go to Plan → During, type your task in, and press Check this. The Planner walks you through the rest.
  • Then start revising your task from both reads: your Task Tester first, the Planner second.
