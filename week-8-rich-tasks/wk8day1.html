Week 8 · First session — Studio

Act II · the During · what makes a task rich? · solve three, then find your own
Today's goals: Solve three rich tasks from three different places, and say what makes each one rich. Then find a task that could help answer your research question. That task is the During of your lesson, and you bring it to our next class.

0 · Housekeeping (~5 min)
  • From your Concept Profiles: Sample student work (private link).
  • The written guidelines come first. If something I said in class differs from an assignment's guidelines, follow the guidelines. When in doubt, check the assignment.

1 · Where we're going (~5 min)
  • Your research question is in. Today you look for a task that could help answer it: the During of your lesson.
  • For the next three weeks, you'll test each other's work, one piece of your lesson at a time.
    • Week 8 · Task Tester: solve a classmate's rich task (their During) without having seen it first.
    • Week 9 · Routine Tester: try a classmate's warm-up routines (their Before).
    • Week 10 · Plan Tester: read a classmate's whole Before–During–After plan.
  • Where to find tasks — four places only: NRICH, YouCubed, Van de Walle, chapters 3 and 4 in Canvas (Hamilton · Oxford), and articles on JSTOR from journals like Teaching Children Mathematics, which often show real student work. No Teachers Pay Teachers, and nothing that isn't from one of these four.
  • The six things a rich task does are also in the Rich Task assignment. Keep the page open. After each task today, we check it against that list.

2 · Bowls on Two Tables — Van de Walle (~20 min)
  • The task (kindergarten–grade 1): Bowls on Two Tables (handout).
  • Solve it (10 min) with a partner. Use counters and the two tables on the handout.
  • Check it (10 min): Which of the six things on the rich-task list did this task do? If this were the During of a kindergarten lesson, what would it show you about how a child thinks?

3 · How Close to 100? — YouCubed (~20 min)
  • The game (grades 3–5): How Close to 100? (handout).
  • While we're on YouCubed: their whole task collection.
  • Play it (15 min) with a partner, on the handout or on screen.
  • Check it (5 min): Which of the six things on the rich-task list did it do? What would it show you about how a child thinks, if it were your During?

4 · Scavenger hunt — find a task for your question (~20 min)
  • Start on NRICH (15 min). Go to the primary collection and find the mathematics your research question is about. NRICH is British, so its year levels are one higher than our grades: a US grade 3 is "Year 4." Many NRICH problems have a Solutions page with work sent in by real students, which helps next class.
  • If a task from Van de Walle, YouCubed, or a JSTOR article fits your question better, use it.
  • Test each candidate with three questions: Does it fit my research question? Could a child at my grade get started on it? Can it be solved at least three different ways? If the answer to any of them is no, keep looking.
  • Post your best candidate (5 min): its title, where you found it, and a link.
Debrief & homework (~10 min):
  • A few people share the task they found, and why it fits their question.
  • Before our next class, pick one task and bring it. Don't solve it yet: next class, your Task Testers solve it without having seen it, and you watch how they think. Bring whatever solutions or teacher notes your source has, if any.
  • Skim Van de Walle chapter 3 (Hamilton · Oxford) for what makes a problem worth solving. Save chapter 4 for next week, when you build the Before.
  • This task is the During of the lesson you'll build over the next three weeks. See the Rich Task assignment.
