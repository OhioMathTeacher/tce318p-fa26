Week 2 · Second session — Clinic

Number & Base-Ten · Listener → Questioner · listening to thinking on paper
Today's goals: Turn your listening onto paper. After comparing last class's solutions and the hundred-chart reading, work the hundred chart as a questioning task — then read one child's written place-value thinking, diagnose it, and ask questions that open it up.

0 · Quinn's project (~10 min)

  • Quinn joins us to explain his project and hand out consent forms.
  • His survey is in tonight's homework, at the bottom of this page.

1 · Debrief the tasks (~10 min)

  • Compare the Blue and Green solutions from last class: how did you see it? who saw it differently?
  • We won't debrief the Hundred Chart Challenge reading — we enact it. What Switzer does with a class is what we do next: same chart, same never-tell questioning, with you on both sides of it.
  • In Journaler, keep notes on every assigned reading as you go. That's what I'm looking for when I collect the Reading Journal — all five entries, due Sunday, September 27 on Canvas.

Purpose: settle last class quickly — today's whole middle is the reading, so the reading gets lived instead of discussed.

2 · Hundred Chart Challenge (~30 min)

  • Groups of three — three Challenge cards between us, so three or four groups work the same one. All three cards run on the same two numbers, so what changes is the question, not the numbers. Work your card together: how would you solve it on the hundred chart?
  • One group per card takes the fishbowl — three in all. As they share, the rest of the class asks open questions to draw out their reasoning — never tell. If you worked that card, you lead the questioning.
  • Last five minutes: the three cards, one answer. What was the same, and what did the chart make you do differently?

Purpose: counting back and counting up are opposite journeys to the same difference — put them on the same two numbers and place value is what's left to talk about. Card 1's wording is the wording you'll meet again in the Excursion.

3 · Pedagogical Excursion 2 (~30 min)

  • Now on paper: read one child's written work and respond.
  • In class, no AI — on your own or with one partner. Collected at the end.

Purpose: on paper you can't probe — you must infer. Reading a child before you respond is the Listener's core work.

If you finish early

  • Of your questions for the child, which one would you open with — and why is it the strongest first move?
Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • Complete Quinn's survey — no more than ten minutes.
  • Complete the Week 2 Reading Homework.
  • Next week: you step from Questioner to Poser — you run the math talks and pose the questions yourself.
