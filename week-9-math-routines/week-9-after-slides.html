The After

Your Before and During leave evidence too. The After is where you ask for it on purpose. The danger: an After that is easy to collect and tells you nothing about your question.

last class’s task, one more time
The task (the During)
Here are 12 counters. Show me 3/4 of them, and tell me how you know it’s 3/4.
Research question: How do students understand a fraction of a group?
Standard: 3.NF.1, understand a/b as a parts of size 1/b.

The lesson is over. Next: six Afters someone might plan. In your group, decide: would students’ responses to your After provide evidence for this question, on this math, or does it miss? Be prepared to share your conclusion and your evidence.

Check: what each After captures
  • 1 · Thumbs up. A room of nods. Nothing to read later, and nothing about how.
  • 2 · 3/4 of 20. Right math, but it keeps only the answer. The question asks how.
  • 3 · A fraction equal to 3/4. Real thinking, about the wrong math. That is equivalence (3.NF.3), not a fraction of a group.
  • 4 · The three clearest solutions. Whose thinking? The clearest solutions may not be the ones that show how students understand a fraction of a group. Choose for your question, not for clarity.
  • 5 · Copy the rule. The After became a reteach. It captures the teacher’s thinking, not the students’.
  • 6 · The probe. New numbers, the same move, and a reason in writing.
The principle
A good After provides evidence of students’ understanding of the concept your question names, on the same mathematics as your task and your standards.
The one question to carry
Would students’ responses provide evidence for your research question? In what way(s)?
