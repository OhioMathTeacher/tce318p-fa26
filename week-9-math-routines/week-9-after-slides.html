The After

Your Before and During leave evidence too. The After is where you ask for it on purpose. The danger: an After that is easy to collect and tells you nothing about your question.

What we argued about
  • Thumbs up. A room of nods. Nothing to read later, and nothing about how.
  • 3/4 of 20. Right math, but it keeps only the answer. The question asks how.
  • A fraction equal to 3/4. Real thinking, about the wrong math. That is equivalence (3.NF.3), not a fraction of a group.
  • The three clearest solutions. Whose thinking? Rosa and Jaylen may not be on the board. Choose for your question, not for clarity.
  • Copy the rule. The After became a reteach. It captures the teacher’s thinking, not the students’.
  • The probe. New numbers, the same move, a focus student, and a reason in writing.
The principle
A good After leaves you holding how students think, on the same mathematics as your task and your standards, from the students your question is about.
The one question to carry
If a student answered your After, would that answer tell you something about your research question?

Now build yours. Then a classmate plays the student.
