Week 9 · First session — Studio

Act II · design the Before · how do you warm up the thinking without giving the task away?
Today's goals: Design the Before of your lesson — a short routine that opens on the same mathematics your task investigates and surfaces students' entry thinking. First we judge real routines against a shared task — does each warm up the thinking, or give the task away? Then you type one Before for your own rich task into the BDA Planner and show it to a classmate.

0 · Where we're going (~5 min)
  • The rest of Act II on one page: Where We're Going →

1 · Before, or give-away? — judging routines (~25 min)
  • Your rich task is the During — the main event. The Before is a brief, low-floor warm-up on the same concept that surfaces how students already think. The danger is the pull to help: a routine that hands over the strategy, picture, or procedure the task is meant to reveal gives the task away.
  • Together we walk four real routines against one shared task. For each: does it warm up the thinking, or hand the task away? Argue it — some clearly prime, some clearly give it away, some are worth a fight.
  • We land one principle: a good Before warms up the same mathematics and surfaces entry thinking, but withholds the task's move — so the task can still reveal what students bring.

Purpose: you already sense when a warm-up "gives it away." Today you name what makes the difference. You ran these routines in Week 3 as a participant; now you judge them as a diagnostician.

2 · Your turn — build your Before and try it (~50 min)
  • Consider (~10 min). Open the routines library and go to the shelf for your concept — number talks, Which One Doesn't Belong?, Same & Different, Notice & Wonder, estimation, and more. Find one routine that could warm up your rich task without giving it away.
  • Develop (~20 min). Type it into the BDA Planner — Plan → Before. Run it past the test: could a student still show me something of their own on the task, or did this warm-up already do its work?
  • A classmate looks (~15 min). Share your task and name the move it depends on. Then show your classmate your routine. Does it surface entry thinking? Does it give the move away? Then swap.
  • The genAI planning partner (~5 min). Press Check this on your Before, then follow up with Ask me. Out of time? Do it at home.

Purpose: your task is the filter. A routine earns its place as your Before only if it primes the thinking your task investigates — and leaves the task's move for students to make.

Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • Before our next class, revise your routine from both reads: your classmate's and the genAI planning partner's. If you didn't get to Check this and Ask me in class, do them at home.
  • Next class we design the After. See the Math Routine assignment.
