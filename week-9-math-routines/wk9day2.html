Week 9 · Second session — Studio

Act II · design the After · how will you capture what students' thinking leaves behind?
Today's goals: Design the After of your lesson: the piece you design to capture students' thinking. First, in six groups, we judge six Afters against last class's task. Does each one provide evidence for the research question, on the math in the standard? Then you build your own After in the BDA Planner and try it on a classmate.

1 · Capture, or miss? Judging Afters (~25 min)
  • Count off by 6. Your group judges one After in the slides, then presents it.
  • The big idea: a good After provides evidence of students' understanding of the concept your question names.

2 · The After in the BDA Planner (~5 min)
  • Before you build, I show you two things on the screen in the BDA Planner: the After editor (Plan → After), and where Check this is, under your After on the Plan page.
  • Reload the Planner first. Check this now reads your After against the Investigation Plan rubric, so if your exit slip has only two Student Questions, expect it to ask for a third.

3 · Your turn (~30 min)

The goal: leave with an After that would show you something about your research question.

  • Build your After in the BDA Planner.
  • Swap with a classmate. They play the student and answer your questions. Ask yourself: does that answer tell me anything about my research question? Then switch.
  • Press Check this under your After. Revise tonight.
Wrap-up & looking ahead (~10 min):
  • Before our next class, revise your After from both reads: your classmate's and the genAI planning partner's.
  • Before next class, get your plan ready to share. In the BDA Planner, go to Share → Feedback copy (Word). Upload it to your Google Drive and open it with Google Docs. Bring the link.
  • The Math Routine one-pager is due Sunday.
  • Next class, a classmate reads your whole lesson (Before, During, and After) against your research question.
  • A first look ahead: the Final Report, due December 9. You'll discuss at least three pieces of evidence, and each one needs its raw artifact in an appendix.
