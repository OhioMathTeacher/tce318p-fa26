Week 9 · Second session — Studio

Act II · design the After · how will you capture what students' thinking leaves behind?
Today's goals: Design the After of your lesson: the piece you design to capture students' thinking. First, in six groups, we judge six Afters against last class's task. Does each one leave you holding evidence for the research question, on the math in the standard? Then you build your own After in the BDA Planner and try it on a classmate.

1 · Capture, or miss? Judging Afters (~25 min)
  • The After is how you capture students' thinking on purpose: a short record of their thinking that you keep, plus a question or two that pushes the reasoning. Your Before and During leave evidence too (board photos, your notes, student work). The After is where you ask for it.
  • Count off by 6. Your group takes one of six Afters for last class's task: After 1 · After 2 · After 3 · After 4 · After 5 · After 6. Capture, or miss? Agree on one sentence that says why, then one of you presents it to the room (~8 min in groups, ~2 min per group).
  • The big idea: a good After leaves you holding evidence of students' understanding of the concept your question names, on the same math as your task and your standards.

Purpose: the After is the most-skipped, least-planned piece of the lesson. An After can capture plenty of thinking and still miss, if it is not the thinking your question asks about.

2 · The After in the BDA Planner (~5 min)
  • Before you build, I show you two things on the screen: the After editor (Plan → After), and where Check this is, under your After on the Plan page.
  • Reload the Planner first: the build number should say 88. Check this now reads your After against the Investigation Plan rubric, so if your exit slip has only two Student Questions, expect it to ask for a third.

3 · Your turn (~40 min)

The goal: leave with an After that would show you something about your research question.

  • Build your After (~20 min) in the BDA Planner.
  • Swap with a classmate (~15 min). They play the student and answer your questions. Ask yourself: does that answer tell me anything about my research question? Then switch.
  • Press Check this (~5 min) under your After. Revise tonight.
Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • Before our next class, revise your After from both reads: your classmate's and the genAI planning partner's.
  • The Math Routine one-pager is due Sunday.
  • Next class, a classmate reads your whole lesson (Before, During, and After) against your research question.
  • A first look ahead: the Final Report, due December 9. You'll discuss at least three pieces of evidence, and each one needs its raw artifact in an appendix. Three things to do now, while you finish planning:
    • Keep your raw notes as you wrote them: the index card, the notebook page, the screenshot from your phone.
    • Copy student work before it goes back, with names redacted.
    • Photograph board work before it's erased.
