Week 9 · Second session — Studio

Act II · design the After · how will you capture what students' thinking leaves behind?
Today's goals: Design the After of your lesson: the piece you design to capture students' thinking. First we judge six Afters against last class's task. Does each one leave you holding evidence for the research question, on the math in the standard? Then you build your own After in the BDA Planner and generate your whole lesson as one document.

1 · Capture, or miss? Judging Afters (~25 min)
  • The After is how you capture students' thinking on purpose: a short artifact you physically collect, plus a question or two that pushes the reasoning. Your Before and During leave evidence too (board photos, your notes, student work). The After is where you ask for it.
  • The Planner offers four kinds of After: an exit slip (one to three prompts on paper), a discussion transcript (questions you ask in the closing discussion, with what students actually say), a strategy gallery (chosen solutions on the board, photographed and annotated), and a one-on-one probe (three questions for one focus student).
  • Together we walk six Afters against last class's task. For each: capture, or miss? Some are easy calls and some are worth a fight. Argue it.
  • The big idea: a good After leaves you holding how students think, on the same math as your task and your standards, from the students your question is about.

Purpose: the After is the most-skipped, least-planned piece of the lesson. An After can capture plenty of thinking and still miss, if it is not the thinking your question asks about.

2 · Your turn: build your After and check it (~35 min)
  • Develop (~20 min). Type your After into the BDA Planner: Plan → After. Pick the kind, then write the artifact you'll collect and the question(s) that push the thinking. Keep your Profile open beside it so your research question and standards are in view.
  • A classmate looks (~15 min). Share your research question and your standards. Then show your classmate your After and run the three checks:
    • The question. Would a student's response answer your research question, or only the task?
    • The standards. Is it the same math as your standards, or has it drifted next door?
    • The students. Will your focus students' thinking end up in your hands?
    Then swap.

Purpose: your research question is the filter. An After works only if a student's response could answer it.

3 · The genAI planning partner (~10 min)
  • Press Check this on your After, then follow up with Ask me. It reads your research question and standards too. Out of time? Do it at home.
  • The genAI planning partner is a critic, not a designer. Keep what is useful and Set aside what is not.

4 · See the whole lesson (~10 min)
  • You now have a Before, a During, and an After. In the Planner, go to Share → Generate Investigation Plan — Word. You get one .docx with your research question, standards in full, objectives, materials, and the whole lesson from Before to After.
  • Open it in Word, or upload it to Google Drive and open it with Google Docs. You can keep editing there.
  • Read it top to bottom. Do the Before, the During, and the After still line up with your question and your standards? Any empty box is something still to write.

Purpose: you built the pieces one at a time. Today is the first time you read them as one lesson.

Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • Before our next class, revise your After from both reads: your classmate's and the genAI planning partner's. Then generate the Word file again and read the whole lesson once more.
  • The Math Routine one-pager is due Sunday.
  • Next class, a classmate reads your whole lesson (Before, During, and After) against your research question.
