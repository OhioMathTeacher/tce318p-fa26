Week 3 · Wednesday — Studio + Clinic

Geometry, 2.G / 3.G attributes · Poser · the same figure seen many ways, then named
Today's goals: Labor Day takes Monday, so Week 3 runs as one session. Live two What Do You See? / How Do You Know? geometry talks and feel how one figure gets seen many ways — "diamond," "rhombus," "parallelogram." Then put a name to that spread: van Hiele levels. The spread isn't error, it's level — and hearing the level in how someone reasons is the diagnostic move this whole week is built on.

1 · Two geometry talks — you present (~40 min · ~20 each)

  • We run each routine together — flash a figure, "What do you see?", collect a few, then "How do you know?" Ask, never tell.
  • WODB Which One Doesn't Belong? — four figures; pick one that doesn't belong and defend it (every figure can be defended). Slides →
  • Same & Different — two figures; say what's the same and what's different. Pick a pair from Same But Different — Geometry (28 pairs, each with a full-size view for projecting), or use these three.
  • Keep your own notes on what you heard people say — you'll need them in twenty minutes.

Purpose: two routines still surface the one thing that matters — how differently people see the same form. You have to hear the spread yourself before it can have a name.

2 · Name the levels (~15 min)

  • First, say it out loud: what did you just hear? Name two answers to the same figure that were both defensible.
  • Then the levels go on the board — Level 0 names by appearance ("a diamond"), Level 1 reasons from properties ("equal sides, straight, closed"), Level 2 relates them ("a square is a special rectangle").
  • Go back to what you wrote down in the talks. Where do those answers sit?

Purpose: what you heard in the talks wasn't error — it was level. Naming it is the first diagnostic move. Because we're doing this in one session, say what you heard before we label it, so the naming lands on something you already noticed.

3 · Run nVoke, then locate the level (~25 min)

  • As a class, open nVoke and run the van Hiele Geometry pack in Diagnostic mode. Each of you types what you see for every figure and keeps your own responses. Ask every question about the app now, so it's second nature when you pose it yourself.
  • Read your own responses through the levels — where did you land? (You won't all be Level 2.)
  • Swap with a partner and locate theirs from the reasoning, not the label. Locate, don't rank — a "diamond" answer is data, not an error.

Purpose: the class becomes one dataset, and nVoke does no analysis — you do the diagnosing. The pick tells you little; the How do you know? is the diagnosis.

  • Sort Run the third routine on your own: take a handful of figures, group them, and state your rule.
  • Take a response you'd call Level 0 and write the one question you'd ask to invite the next level — without telling.
