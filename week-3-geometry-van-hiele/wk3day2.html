Week 3 · Second session — Clinic

Geometry, 2.G / 3.G attributes · Poser · listening for the level
Today's goals: Put a name to last class's spread — van Hiele levels — and learn to hear the level in how a learner reasons, not just what they name. The aim is to locate, not to rank: you diagnose in order to offer the right next experience.

1 · Name the levels (~20 min)
  • Debrief Newell & Orton — the routines you ran Tuesday, now described from the teacher’s side. Then the ladder goes on the board:
    • Level 1 · Visual names a shape by how it looks ("a diamond," "a door")
    • Level 2 · Analysis reasons from properties ("four equal sides, square corners")
    • Level 3 · Abstraction relates them ("a square is a special rectangle"). Keep the levels reference sheet beside you — it is the same ladder the app shows you.
  • Connect the spread you saw last class to these levels — the same spread, now with a name.

2 · Swap laptops — then both of you answer (~15 min)
  • Swap first. Hand your laptop to your partner and take theirs. You will answer on their machine.
  • Open nVoke → van Hiele Geometry → Handoff · one person — the whole-room setting keeps nothing. Four cards. Both of you answer at the same time.
  • Type your real reasoning, not what you think I want to read. Someone is about to diagnose it.
  • Field every question about the app now — you pose it yourself on Sunday.
  • The last screen tells you to hand the laptop back. Do that — the Doer never analyses.

3 · Swap back — locate the level, then ask (~20 min)
  • Take your own laptop back. Your partner’s answers are sitting on it.
  • Go to Analyze and work alone first — don’t talk to your partner yet. Name the level, click an answer to attach their exact words as evidence, and say why those words put them there. The app makes you quote, because the quote is the diagnosis.
  • Now turn to them and ask. "How do you know?" — ask, never tell. Where did you read them wrong?
  • Then say what you’d offer them next, pitched at their level — not above it.

4 · Export once, and open the Excursion (~10 min)
  • Export the PDF right now, from the transcript you just analysed — if today’s answers are good enough to read, that file is what you hand in. It prints from the app; there is nothing to download and nothing to unblock.
  • We open Pedagogical Excursion #3 together and read it through — that is the one place the details live.
  • Close: how did naming the levels change what you heard?

5 · Build your own (~15 min)
  • In nVoke: ＋ New pack → ＋ Add card → 💾 Save pack, and ⬇ Export pack (.json) to keep it.
  • Pick figures that are close to each other — square, tilted square, rhombus, rectangle. Pick a star, a heart and a circle instead and students will answer by how things look, which tells you nothing about where they are.
  • Excursion #3 lets you build your own instead of using the built-in pack — this is how.
Wrap-up & looking ahead:
  • Read Higgins, “Building a Team of Learners” now — Higgins’s teachers do exactly what you just did: sit with a child’s own words and name the level. You met the levels in the room first, on purpose.
  • Turn in Pedagogical Excursion #3.
  • Write your Week 3 entry in Journaler now, under This Week’s Entry — the five entries are collected together after Week 5, not this week.
  • Next week: from Poser to Planner — anticipating how students will think so you can plan the discussion.
