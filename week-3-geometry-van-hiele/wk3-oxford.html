Week 3 · Wednesday — Build · Handoff · Analyze

Geometry, 2.G / 3.G attributes · Poser · pose a task, then read what comes back
Today's goals: Labor Day takes Monday, so Week 3 runs as one session — and we spend it doing the whole of Pedagogical Excursion #3 once, together, before you do it for real. Three phases in nVoke: Build a task, Hand off to a partner, Analyze what they said. This is Thinker–Doer from Week 2 with an instrument attached — the Thinker poses and never tells, the Doer thinks out loud. What's new is that you now have a name for what you're hearing: van Hiele levels.

1 · I run all three phases in front of you (~20 min)

  • I build a short prompt pack, pose it to the room, and then read your answers out loud through the levels — the entire Excursion #3 arc in one pass, so you've seen the destination before you start.
  • The levels go on the board while I'm analysing, not before: Level 1 · Visual describes a shape by its appearance ("a diamond"), Level 2 · Analysis describes it by its properties ("equal sides, square corners"), Level 3 · Abstraction relates those properties ("a square is a special rectangle"). We number them 1–5, the way Howse & Howse do in this week's reading. They're in the app too, next to the claim you make.
  • Ask every question about nVoke now. Once you hand your laptop over, you want the tool to be invisible.

Purpose: you see the finished move before you attempt it. The levels arrive exactly when they become useful — the moment someone has to say where a real answer sits.

2 · Build your own prompt pack (~20 min)

  • In Build, make three or four cards using the routines you already know: WODB four figures, one you can argue out · Same & Different a pair · Sort a handful to group and rule.
  • Aim your figures at the boundary that actually splits levels — square, tilted square, rhombus, rectangle, parallelogram. A star next to a heart only gets you appearance talk.
  • Test of a good card: the answer alone won't tell you who's where, but the reasoning will.
  • Save it. You'll pose this one today and submit it Sunday.

Purpose: writing the task is the Poser's real work. You find out what your card is worth only when someone answers it.

3 · Pose 1 — you are the Thinker (~20 min)

  • Hand your laptop to your partner and set nVoke to Handoff. They type; you sit beside them and ask "How do you know?" — ask, never tell. Their answers stay on your machine.
  • Take it back and go to Analyze. Pick the levels you can see, then click their answers to attach them as evidence — the app makes you quote, because the quote is the diagnosis. Then say what you'd offer them next, pitched at their level.
  • Your partner watches you do it. Where do they think you read them wrong?

Purpose: locate, don't rank. A "diamond" answer is data, not an error — and the label they gave tells you almost nothing next to the justification.

4 · Pose 2 — now you are the Doer (~20 min)

  • Swap. Their laptop, their pack, their questions — you answer, they read you.
  • Being on the other side is the point: it's the only way to feel what it's like to have your reasoning located by someone else.
  • Close: what did your partner hear in your answers that you didn't know you'd said?

Purpose: the Doer never analyses their own work. You learn what the transcript reveals by being the one inside it.

If you finish early

  • Take an answer you'd call Level 1 and write the one question you'd ask to invite the next level — without telling.
  • Run the third routine on your own in Sort Lab: group a handful of figures, state your rule, then sort them a different way.
