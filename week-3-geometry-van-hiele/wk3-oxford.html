Week 3 · Wednesday — Pose, Listen, Locate

Geometry, 2.G / 3.G attributes · Poser · pose a task, then read what comes back
Today's goals: Labor Day takes Monday, so Week 3 runs as one session — and we spend it doing the whole of Pedagogical Excursion #3 once, together, before you do it for real. You pose a task in nVoke, hand the laptop over, and then read what your partner said. This is Thinker–Doer from Week 2 with an instrument attached — the Thinker poses and never tells, the Doer thinks out loud. What's new is that you now have a name for what you're hearing: van Hiele levels.

1 · Your five questions, read back to you (~15 min)

  • I hand back Pedagogical Excursion 2. Photograph only question 2 — your five questions. Not the whole sheet: my marks are on it, and a model that can see a grade starts agreeing with the grade instead of reading your work.

I'm training to be a teacher. A third grader solved 83 − 18 as 8 − 3 = 5 and 8 − 1 = 7, getting 75 (subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column). The attached photo shows five questions I wrote to ask this student. Please don't re-diagnose the student, and don't write better questions for me. For each of my five, tell me whether it is genuinely open or whether it funnels toward the answer I already have in mind — and quote the words in my question that made you say so.

  • Read what it pushed you on. Which of yours actually funnel? Would you rewrite one?
  • Now finish the move: pick the one question you'd open with, and say in a sentence why it fits this child — this misconception, not a general prompt.
  • Around the room — where did genAI help, and where did it miss?
  • Not here on the 2nd? Sit with a partner and work theirs. Nothing is collected today.

Purpose: you spend the rest of today asking a partner questions. Getting your own questioning read back to you first is the shortest route to hearing yourself do it.

2 · The levels — and one card, posed to the room (~10 min)

  • The ladder goes on the board: Level 1 · Visual names a shape by how it looks ("a diamond," "a door") · Level 2 · Analysis reasons from properties ("four equal sides, square corners") · Level 3 · Abstraction relates them ("a square is a special rectangle"). Keep the levels reference sheet beside you — it is the same ladder the app shows you.
  • I pose one card from the built-in van Hiele Geometry pack and we locate two or three of your answers together, out loud.
  • Notice the figure stays on screen. Geometry cards don't flash — the reasoning only exists while you can still see the shape.
  • Ask every question about nVoke now. Once you hand your laptop over, you want the tool to be invisible.

Purpose: the levels arrive the moment they become useful — when someone has to say where a real answer sits. You meet them in the room first, and read Higgins tonight.

3 · Swap laptops — then both of you answer (~15 min)

  • Swap first. Hand your laptop to your partner and take theirs. You will answer on their machine.
  • Open nVoke → van Hiele Geometry → Handoff · one person. Four cards. Both of you answer at the same time.
  • Type your real reasoning, not what you think I want to read. Someone is about to diagnose it.
  • When you reach the last screen it tells you to hand the laptop back. Do that — the Doer never analyses.

Purpose: swapping first is what puts each transcript on the machine that has to read it. Answering at the same time is forced by the shared pack — go one at a time on identical cards and the second person already knows every question.

4 · Swap back — locate the level, then ask (~20 min)

  • Take your own laptop back. Your partner's answers are sitting on it.
  • Go to Analyze and work alone first — don't talk to your partner yet. Name the level, click an answer to attach their exact words as evidence, and say why those words put them there. The app makes you quote, because the quote is the diagnosis.
  • Now turn to them and ask. "How do you know?" — ask, never tell. Where did you read them wrong, and what did they mean instead?
  • Then say what you'd offer them next, pitched at their level — not above it.

Purpose: locate, don't rank. A "diamond" answer is data, not an error. Committing to a level before you can ask is the whole exercise — on Sunday the learner won't be sitting there to correct you.

5 · The other routines in nVoke (~10 min)

  • Not everything in the app is geometry. Ten-Frame Flash and Dot Patterns run as a room: the dots appear, then vanish, then — "How many did you see? How did you see it?"
  • Different ladder, same move: Count by ones · Subitize a group · Compose / decompose. Locating a level is a move, not a van Hiele trick.
  • This is also where the flash belongs. You hide dots because the whole question is what you grabbed in that instant; you leave a figure up because its reasoning dies the moment it's gone.
  • These are not for Excursion #3 — that one is geometry.

Purpose: the ear you built on shapes is not about shapes. It transfers the moment the ladder changes underneath it.

6 · Export once, and open the Excursion (~10 min)

  • Export the PDF right now, from the transcript you just analysed — so Sunday is not the first time you try it. It prints from the app; there is nothing to download and nothing to unblock.
  • We open Pedagogical Excursion #3 together and read it through — that is the one place the details live.
  • ⚠ The one thing Sunday does differently: today your Doer typed their own words. On Sunday you type — the learner's words, exactly as said, not your paraphrase. Today never rehearses that, so know it now.
  • Two cautions: pose in Handoff · one person (the whole-room setting keeps nothing), and export before you close the tab.
  • Close: what did your partner hear in your answers that you didn't know you'd said?

If you finish early

  • Take an answer you'd call Level 1 and write the one question you'd ask to invite the next level — without telling.
  • Run the third routine in Sort Lab: group a handful of figures, state your rule, then sort them a different way and see whether the rule survives.
