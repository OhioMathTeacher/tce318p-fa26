Week 5 · Second session — Clinic

Assessment · rubrics, debriefed · the field investigation, in one picture · Assessor · one learner's work, on your own
Today's goals: Debrief McGatha & Darcy against the rubrics you scored with. Open the field investigation: the back half of the course, built on one mathematical concept and the students learning it. Then do the Assessor's move on your own — read one real learner's work, name its DOK, and write the question you would ask next.

1 · Reading debrief — McGatha & Darcy (~15 min)

  • Your journal prompt, out loud: what does an assessment make visible, what does it hide, and how do we use it for a learner, not just about them?
  • Last class you sorted rubrics into the ones you'd reach for to decide what to teach next and the ones that only hand you a grade. How does McGatha sort them? Put our three rubrics where she would.
  • Rubric 3 (Charles, Lester & O'Daffer) is McGatha's Figure 3. Check your table's read of it against hers.

Purpose: close the studio before the course turns to real students — McGatha gives the pattern you built on the board its names.

2 · Field investigation overview (~30 min)

  • The back half of the course is one field investigation: one mathematical concept, and two or three students whose work shows how they understand it. Concept → question → design → collect → analyze → share.
  • The shape of it. On the course home page, Act II and Act III lay out every piece — what it is, what it's worth, and when it's due. We walk that table together.
  • The Week 6 assignment. We read the Concept Profile guidelines together now, so you walk into your field dip knowing what to bring back: with your host teacher, choose one concept, find your two or three focus students, and collect three to five pieces of their actual work.
  • Where you write it. The Concept Profile is written in the BDA Planner and submitted to Canvas as a PDF. It is one file from Week 6 to Week 10 — the profile first, then the lesson you plan from it.

3 · One learner's work — a Misconception Quick Response (~30 min)

  • Your second Misconception Quick Response — in class, on your own, no AI, collected as you finish. Everything you need is in Pedagogical Excursion #5.

Purpose: last class you explored the lens with your table; now you point it yourself, at one real learner's work. That is the Assessor's move.

If you finish the MQR early

  • Swap with a neighbor and read their questions. Which one best invites the student's own reasoning — without nudging them toward the answer? Say why.
